The problem isn't that you don't know the answer

You fold, he shows a bluff, and you spend the drive home deciding you're unlucky. You call, he has it, and you decide you're a calling station. Neither is a lesson, because in both cases the only thing you learned was the card.

What you needed was the count: how many of the hands he can hold actually beat yours, and what price you were being offered. That number exists. Nobody ever shows it to you.

Where the numbers come from

This is the part that matters, so it's worth being exact about which bits are arithmetic and which are judgement.

Counting: exact
Every hand he can hold is enumerated and compared to yours, card by card. "592 of his 990" is a count, not an estimate. Chops are counted separately and said out loud.
The money: measured
Each option is played to the end of the hand 250 times by a real poker engine, with him dealt a fresh hand from that same range every time. The dollar figures are what actually happened, averaged — not a formula.
The opponent: a model
He's a simulated player type, and you're told which one before you act, because the same cards against someone who never folds and someone who never calls have opposite right answers. It's labelled a model because that's what it is.
The coaching: written
The sentences are written by us over the measured numbers. They're labelled as written so you know which part is arithmetic and which part is a human being making a call.

Every hand carries those four labels on screen. If a number can't be defended, it doesn't ship — the build refuses it.

Fair questions

Is this a GTO trainer?

No, and deliberately. Solver output is built for players studying balanced ranges against other studied players. At $1/$2 the money comes from ordinary mistakes against ordinary opponents, so the coaching is about what actually beats the player in front of you.

How do I know the numbers aren't made up?

You don't have to take it on trust — the counts are shown broken down by hand type, so you can check them against the board yourself. Every build re-derives every claim from the cards independently of the code that produced it, and fails if anything disagrees.

Will this work if I've never studied poker?

It's written for someone who plays and wants to think better, not for someone learning the rules. If you know what a flush beats, you can use it. There is no jargon you have to look up.

Can I use it while I'm playing?

No. It's a study tool, the hands are pre-computed, and using anything like this during a live hand is cheating in most rooms. That's why every screen says study only.

Hands are dealt by a simulation and scored by replaying them. Counts are exact; opponent behaviour is a model; the coaching lines are written.
